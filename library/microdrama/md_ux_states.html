2

Help — the trust surface

The hardest question in this flow isn't "how does this work", it's "is it safe to give ₹10 to a TV channel, and what if it goes wrong". People don't ask that out loud — they just don't tap. Answering it visibly, before payment, is a conversion feature.

Help panelFive real questions, in the order they occur. The last one — the failure path — is the one that earns the tap: a visible way to reach a human signals accountability.

The help control sits quietly on the promo surface, then moves next to the pay button once the sheet is up — placed where the doubt actually happens.

3

The offer — two steps, no surprises

The old single sheet implied you keep watching right here, then redirected you to an app store. A surprise redirect after taking someone's money is the fastest way to burn trust. So the destination is named before payment, and the app step gets its own screen with a button rather than an automatic jump.

Step 2 — the handoffA tapped button, never an automatic redirect: auto-jumping to an app store reads as malware. It also states exactly where they'll land, so the next screen confirms a promise instead of surprising them.

5

The returning user's screen — TV plays, the sachet store sits under it

Wiom TV still starts playing the instant it opens. Borrowing YouTube's vertical-mobile layout, the video pins to the top and the space beneath becomes the sachet store: running packs (only when there are any), a row per app showing what ₹10 buys there and a strip of what is actually inside it, and the last pack bought. People buy what they can see, so every row sells with artwork rather than an app name.

Last packOnly the most recent purchase is kept — a one-tap way back to the thing they already chose, not a ledger to audit. It records what the pack was spent on ("9 शोज़"), which is the receipt that makes buying again feel earned rather than repeated.

Every poster is an entrance to the same loop: tap it and the promo plays exactly as channel 21 does, ending in the same ₹10 offer carrying that app's hours, then the handoff. The store isn't a second product — it is n doors into one machine, which is why adding a provider costs artwork and an hours figure, not a new flow.

One price everywhere, and time is the dial. ₹10 is a decision nobody has to think about twice, so the only variable left is how long it lasts — 12 hours of microdrama, 4 of premium film and sport. Content value is expressed in hours, exactly the way a recharge expresses it in data.

Back at the offerHalf the height of the first-timer sheet — recognition instead of persuasion, and the loop closes where it began.

What all of this is built to measure: the share of first-time payers who buy a second time, and how long it takes them. The balance pill, the standing guide card, the expiry strip and the shortened returner sheet are four separate shots at that one number — instrumented separately, so we learn which one actually earns the second ₹10. That number decides whether this is a business or a one-off novelty.
