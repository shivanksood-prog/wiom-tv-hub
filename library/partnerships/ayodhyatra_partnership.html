Partnership Models & Technical Integration

A working brief for Ayodhyatra — the three ways your devotional content can reach Wiom's audience, what we need from you, and detailed answers to every integration question raised.
Prepared 23 July 2026Surfaces: wiom.tv web app (PWA) + Wiom TV Android app

1 Partnership Models & Our Requirements

Wiom TV is Wiom's live entertainment surface — a web app (PWA) and an Android TV app that reach Wiom's broadband households. There are three ways to bring Ayodhyatra's content to that audience. They differ in how deep the integration goes, but share one principle:

Ayodhyatra keeps its content on its own hosting and retains full control. Wiom adds only the surface, the access layer, or the promotion — no content migration, and no lock-in.
Option 1Channel on Wiom TV
Ayodhyatra runs as a linear channel inside the Wiom TV app, alongside our existing channels. Wiom points its player at your hosted stream; viewers tune in like any other channel.
We need: a hosted 24/7 stream (HLS), channel metadata (name, category, language), and a channel logo.
Option 2Sachet on Wiom TV
A self-contained content pack (e.g. the Ayodhyatra series) is surfaced within Wiom TV and unlocked by the viewer on our surface. Content streams on demand from your hosting; Wiom provides the surface and the access layer.
We need: on-demand episodes (HLS or hosted MP4), pack metadata, and key-art.
Option 3Promo → Install Handshake
Wiom shows a promo for Ayodhyatra; the viewer installs Ayodhyatra's own app; a handshake passes context at install. Your content never flows through Wiom — we drive discovery and installs.
We need: a hosted promo clip, creative assets, your Play Store link, and an install-referrer handshake.

Requirements common to all three

Self-hosted HTTPS only CORS enabled H.264 + AAC-LC HLS preferred 720p cap

RequirementWhat it means
You host itContent and promo stay on your own origin / CDN / object storage. Wiom points at the URL — zero hosting or CDN cost on our side.
HTTPS on every URLOur surfaces are served over HTTPS; non-HTTPS (mixed-content) URLs are blocked by the browser.
CORS enabledYour origin must allow cross-origin playback from wiom.tv. This is required for browser / PWA playback; the native Android app does not need it.
Codec: H.264 + AAC-LCUniversal across both our players. Avoid HEVC (won't play reliably in the web PWA). 720p is the sensible ceiling for our broadband base; ~6s HLS segments.
HLS preferredHLS adapts to bandwidth. A partner-hosted MP4 at a direct URL also works, but it's progressive (no bitrate ladder) — fine for short assets, less so for long-form.

2 Integration Questions — Answered by Option

A · Technical Ingest

QFor content delivery, would you like us to push a feed into your origin, or pull from a manifest URL we host?
O1We pull your hosted .m3u8 and surface it as a linear channel. You keep the origin.
O2We pull your hosted VOD and gate it behind the sachet access layer. You host, we entitle.
O3Nothing streams through us. You host the promo as a VOD asset; our client points at the URL and plays it per-user from the start — zero CDN cost to us — then CTA → install → handshake.
QWhat are the exact codec / container specs — video & audio codec, resolution & bitrate ladder, and segment length?
AllWe accept what you already publish — no re-encode on our side. Target HLS, H.264 + AAC-LC; 720p cap; ~6s segments.
O1Your live / looped HLS as-is.
O2Each episode as HLS on your origin (preferred), or an MP4 hosted on your side at a direct URL. MP4 is progressive — no ladder; HLS adapts down on low bandwidth, so it's better for long-form.
O3No content encode needed — only a short promo VOD you host.
QWhat player technology do you use?
AllWeb (wiom.tv PWA): hls.js v1.5.7 for HLS, with native HLS fallback on Safari/iOS; HTML5 <video> for MP4.  Android TV app: AndroidX Media3 ExoPlayer (HLS + progressive MP4) with segment caching. Deliver H.264 + AAC and it plays on both — no partner SDK required.
O1Your live stream plays directly in both players.
O2Your on-demand episodes play in the same players, behind the access layer.
O3Your promo VOD plays in the same <video> / ExoPlayer surface, per-user from t=0 — already our live pattern — then hands off to your app.
QWhat EPG format do you expect (XMLTV or proprietary), and how often must it update?
O1Static "now-playing" title by default; supply XMLTV only if you want a richer guide — we don't generate one.
O2No EPG — a pack, not a linear guide. You supply title, poster, and episode list once.
O3N/A — it's a promo unit, no channel or guide.
QIs DRM / content protection required, or is unencrypted delivery fine given our FTA setup?
O1Unencrypted, like our existing FTA channels.
O2The access layer is an entitlement gate, not media DRM — your stream stays clear. Apply AES-128 at your origin if you want media protection.
O3N/A for us — all playback and protection happen inside your app.
QDo you have a partner / channel-management API for scheduling updates, or is onboarding manual?
O1One-time manual add; your feed updates flow through automatically because we pull.
O2You manage the pack at your URL; no scheduling API to build.
O3The handshake is the one-time integration; swap the promo file at your URL and it's picked up automatically.

B · Playout Ownership

QFor library / VOD content like Ayodhyatra that loops as a channel — would you handle playout & scheduling, or would you like us to deliver an already-looped 24/7 feed?
O1You deliver the already-looped 24/7 HLS feed; we pull it. We don't build or run a looper.
O2On-demand — the "loop 24/7" problem disappears; viewers play episodes on demand from your hosting.
O3N/A — playout is entirely inside your app.

C · Commercial & Operational

QAre there content / compliance guidelines specific to AI-generated content we should be aware of?
O1You self-certify licensing / originality and carry the "AI-generated" label per India norms; devotional & cultural sensitivity sits with you as the content owner.
O2As O1, plus a one-time content-policy sign-off — no ongoing review pipeline on our side.
O3You self-certify; the promo creative meets ad norms; in-app content compliance is entirely yours.
QWhat are your branding requirements for channel name, logo, and description — and exactly which assets do you need?

You supply assets to our spec; we place them — no design work on our side. Requirements verified against the live web app, the Android app, and our demos:

O1Channel on Wiom TV
  • Hosted HLS URL (.m3u8, the 24/7 feed)
  • Channel name — ≤18 characters (the web banner shows it)
  • Category: Devotional  ·  Language
  • Channel logo (Android surface): transparent PNG, landscape, min 720×540 (1280×720 preferred), <200 KB — renders "fit" in a 150×88 tile and doubles as the Android-TV home poster. Web uses number + name only, no logo.
O2Sachet on Wiom TV
  • Per-episode HLS on your origin (preferred), or a partner-hosted MP4 at a direct URL
  • Pack key-art: 9:16 portrait, 1080×1920
  • Pack title  ·  one-line hook (~60 chars)
  • Episode list — titles + order
O3Promo → Install Handshake
  • Promo VOD hosted by you — HLS-VOD .m3u8 or H.264/AAC MP4 at a direct / signed URL
  • Landscape hero: 16:9, min 1280×720 (+ optional 9:16 portrait)
  • App name + app icon  ·  Play Store link  ·  CTA copy
  • Handshake parameters — install-referrer token / deep-link scheme

We're deliberately holding DAU down while we get the product right — the platform isn't being promoted across our user base yet. Please size for ~1,000 DAU / daily tune-ins, with concurrency well below that. When we open it up further, we'll give you a week's notice to scale.
O1Your origin serves our (currently capped) viewers.
O2Your origin serves only viewers who unlock the pack — a smaller subset.
O3Promo impressions to that same capped audience, converting to installs handed to your app.
QWhat does a typical onboarding timeline look like for a new content partner?
O1Days — add URL + assets, QA, list.
O2~1 week — stand up the pack surface + access layer + assets.
O3~1 week — creative is fast; the handshake needs install-referrer / deep-link testing.
