Dashverse — Intro Call Playbook

Sensing & positioning. Pull, not push. Talk like a person, not a deck.

Starting lines, not a script — react to what they say

⚡ One-screen glance

Hold the frame: you're the distribution they can't easily reach. Sensing fit, relaxed either way. Ask more than you pitch. Fine with a "no."
Flow: light Wiom intro → researched ₹499 question → sachet insight → let them talk → pull out what's redistributable → small reversible ask.
Open: affordable internet for the India that can't afford it → Wiom Net ₹10 unlimited for anyone + RWGKN ₹20/day flexible home net, unlimited, any devices → next-billion users, pay small & flexible → ~1M users/month → Satyam's "being online lets people get ahead."
Bridge: "₹499/month, higher on iOS — am I right? Only paid offering?" → "Our audience lives sachet-style — they'll pay a few rupees a day, not ₹499/month."
The ask: "Pick a few titles, let us test with one pilot group for a few weeks. You keep everything, nothing exclusive. Worth a try?"
Don'ts: over-thank · "we'd love to" · beg for titles · over-explain tech · monologue · bank a reflexive yes · quote splits · fill silence.
1 The Frame ▸

You're not asking for content. You're the distribution into an audience they can't easily reach — people who'll spend a few rupees a day but never commit to ₹499/month. You're here to figure out fit, and you've got other partners already in pilots, so you're relaxed either way. Don't say any of this — just hold it: ask more than you pitch, don't oversell, be fine with a "no."

Internal reminder: They're qualifying to get on the rail. Not the other way around.
2 Opening — introduce Wiom, lightly ▸

Short and warm — who we are, what we do, who for, the why. Not a vision speech.

What we are
"Quick intro to us — Wiom's basically making the internet affordable for the part of India that mostly can't afford it today."
The offering (two things)
"We've got two things really. Wiom Net — unlimited internet for anyone for just ten rupees. And recharge wala ghar ka net — flexible recharges on home internet, starts at twenty rupees for a day, unlimited usage, as many devices as you want."
Who it's for
"Our users are the next set of Indians coming online — they don't think in monthly plans, they pay small and flexible, when they feel like it."
Where we are
"We're at about a million users a month right now. Plan's a lot bigger, but that's where we are today."
Origin (light)
"It started with a simple belief our founder Satyam had — being online is what lets people get ahead, and most of India's still locked out of it. That's the itch we've been scratching."

Then pause — let them react or ask. Don't roll into Wiom TV unless pulled.

3 Bridge to them — researched question + sachet insight ▸

Don't open on their funnel drop-off (too pointed). Open with your homework, as a respectful check.

"Before this, I spent a bit of time on DashReels. From what I could tell, your paid plans start around ₹499 a month, and a bit higher on iOS. Am I right? Is that the only paid content offering you've got, or is there more to it?"

→ Let them confirm/correct. Respectful homework, not a trap.

"Got it. So here's why this is interesting for us. The audience we serve lives life sachet-style — whether it's shampoo, detergent, or their internet recharge, they buy in tiny amounts. A ₹499 monthly commitment just isn't how they spend. But the same person will happily pay a few rupees a day for something they actually like."

Sets up fit cleanly: not "your model fails," but "there's a whole audience priced differently." Let it breathe.

4 Wiom TV — only when pulled ▸

Don't deliver as a block. Drop one beat when the conversation asks.

What it is
"On top of all that, we've started building an entertainment layer for these users — we call it Wiom TV. Free live TV to replace their cable, and short-form stuff on top of it."
Status (underclaim it)
"It's early — genuinely still in pilots. I'd rather not oversell something half-built."
Why now (practical)
"We're testing content over the next month or so — honestly why I wanted to talk now, rather do it while we're actually trying things than in theory later."
The honest interest
"My real question is just — could some of your content work for these users. That's the thing I want to figure out."
5 Discovery — let them reveal ▸

Ask, then go quiet. Reflect back with "it sounds like…"

Open them up
  • "Walk me through how you think about your content — DashReels, the toons, the creation stuff. Where's the energy going right now?"
  • "What's actually working best with your audience — the Indian dramas, the AI ones, the C and K dramas? Anything surprise you?"
Sourcing (your homework, not prying)
  • "How much of this is yours, made in-house, versus stuff you source or plug in from others? Just trying to understand what's even yours to move around."
  • "Sounds like you've actually built a content engine, not just a library — is that right?"
The redistribution reveal (make them propose it)
  • "If you wanted to reach people who'll never pay your ₹499 but would happily watch every day — what part of your catalogue would you even want out there?"
  • "What would have to be true for you to be comfortable with some of your content living on a screen you don't own?"
  • "How do you think about stuff that's already made its money in your funnel and is just sitting there now?"
Reflect it back (pilot becomes their idea)
  • "So it sounds like the C-dramas and maybe a bit of the AI stuff are what you'd be most okay testing first — that right?"
6 The Ask — small test, said casually ▸
"Honestly, the easiest thing — pick a few titles you're okay with, let us put them in front of one pilot group for a few weeks. You keep everything, nothing exclusive, and you get to see how a totally different audience responds. If nothing happens, no harm. If it works, we figure out the rest. Worth a try?"

Timing nudge (only if true, said offhand):

"We're sorting out launch partners over the next few weeks anyway, so the timing kind of works."

If they hesitate:

"Is now a bad time to run something small like this?" easy "no, now's fine"
7 Reactive flow — if they say X, you say Y ▸
"Show me the product / what exactly is Wiom TV?"
"It's early — still in pilots. Two screens, mobile and a TV one that replaces cable. I'd rather not oversell something half-built. What's real today is the audience and the reach — the content layer is what I'm figuring out, which is why I'm here."
They go quiet / "tell me more"
Turn it back: "What's your read — does an audience like that even sound useful to you, or is your whole game the premium funnel?"
They get enthusiastic fast — "let's do it"
Don't bank it. Pin it: "Love that. What would actually be the first thing — which titles, and who on your side would I work with?"
They push on money / "what's the split"
"Happy to get there — but it's premature. Let's see if the audience even responds first. If it works, the economics are an easy conversation."
They're guarded / skeptical
Label it: "Sounds like you've been burned by distribution deals before, or it's just not a priority right now — either's fine. What's the hesitation?" Then listen.
"Let me think about it"
"Totally. Can I ask what you'd want to see to feel good about even a small test?"
8 Objections — the unspoken ones ▸
"You're tiny and early — waste of our time?"
"We are early, fair. That's exactly why it's a small reversible test, not a big bet. We do the work to prove the audience exists, on our cost, you keep every right. Worst case you learn something cheap."
"Microdrama is mobile, not TV."
"You're right it started on mobile — so mobile's your home with us, the TV thing is just upside. You don't have to buy the TV idea to try the mobile pilot."
"Why not just keep it in our own app?"
"Because these people aren't in your app and won't be — they're not paying ₹499. So it's not your audience moving, it's a new one you weren't reaching. Your app keeps your payers."
"Won't this eat our autopay revenue?"
"Shouldn't — different people, different price, different screen. Stuff that's already made its money in your funnel just gets a second life with an audience that was never going to pay you anyway. If anything it's awareness that feeds your premium product later."
9 Landmines ▸
  • Don't over-thank — once is enough.
  • Don't say "we'd love to" / "would you be open to…" — say "here's what I'd suggest."
  • Don't beg for titles — pull them with the §5 questions.
  • Don't over-explain the tech — "early, in pilots" is the whole answer.
  • Don't feature-dump — intro is light; Wiom TV only when pulled.
  • Don't monologue — them talking 60–70%. After ~3 sentences, ask something.
  • Don't chase a yes — the only real yes has a next step attached.
  • Don't quote terms / splits — push commercials to "after the test."
  • Say the million-a-month number once.
  • Don't fill silence — the pull lives in the pause.
