It said nothing about what happens after the first flick, because in every arm the user was trapped in one genre. fse_v3 tests whether making 20 genres reachable changes anything — and whether letting the shelf re-narrow once a preference shows is better than either extreme.

News is being promoted on a secondary after a null primary. That is a real protocol deviation, and fse_v3's own rule 3 says the opposite — the incumbent wins all ambiguity. The secondary is a good one (it survived whale-trimming, winsorization, attended-only and split-half), and shipping it is defensible. It is written into the code comment and the commit body, and ratified as a deliberate deviation (Q1).

Consequence for fse_v3: the News landing is no longer an experiment, it is the product. Every first session lands on the News hero — all three arms, and even users the experiment excludes. The landing is held constant so that nothing but ordering varies between arms.

Section 3

Three arms

Assignment is hash(uid + '|fse_v3') % 3 — sticky, client-side, new uids only. The landing hero is identical in all three.

Arm RC
Pinned control
News landing + within-genre health-gated sort. Exactly what the winning fse_v2 arm serves today, flagged meta.control:1. Do not improve this arm. fse_v2's near-miss was four treatments and no true baseline; RC exists so the answer can be "keep what we have".
Arm RI
Cross-genre interleave
The flat list is rebuilt as a round-robin across genres. Within-genre order is untouched — the health-gated ranking still decides who is #1 of each genre. Interleaving only.
Arm RL
Interleave, then lock
Starts byte-identical to RI, then locks to the genre the user demonstrably sticks in and puts that genre next. Persists across sessions. A compound treatment — declared, not hidden.
i
Arms are labelled RC/RI/RL, not A/B/C, because A, B and C are fse_v1's arm labels and are still sitting in the events table. Any query filtering on variant without also filtering experiment would have silently pooled July's cohort into this one.
Section 4

Arm RI — how the round-robin actually works

  1. Sort within each genre first. The existing health-gated ranking runs unchanged (Wilson-lower-bound stick rate on chosen tunes, broken channels pinned to the bottom of their genre). RI consumes that output — it never reorders inside a genre.
  2. Then deal one channel per genre, in cycle order. News#1, Movies#1, Entertainment#1, Music#1, Devotional#1, News#2, Movies#2, … Cycle order is set by 14-day hold-30 on chosen tunes: News 10.9% → Movies 9.0% → Entertainment 6.5% → Music 6.4% → Devotional 3.6%. Those five hold 176 of 224 channels and 93% of tune volume.
  3. Genre entry gate. A genre only joins the rotation if it has at least one channel that passed the health floor (start-coverage ≥ 0.5, errors/tune ≤ 0.05). A genre whose entire block is condemned is appended after the rotation, never rotated into slot 2.
  4. The Other bucket — the 15 remaining genres merged — takes one slot every third cycle, landing first at slot 16. Leftovers are appended once the cycle genres run dry.
  5. A genre exhausts, the rotation carries on without it. Movies has 12 channels, so from cycle 13 the rotation is four-wide, then three, and News (76 channels) runs longest.
!
Spec correction. spec_6 describes the Other genres as "1–4 channels each". They are not: DD has 18 and Undefined has 11, so Other is ~50 channels — 22% of the catalogue. Thin rotation weight is still the right call, but for a different reason than the spec gives: those genres earn only 7.1% of tunes and 4.3% of watch minutes, at a 5.4% hold-30 against the cycle-five's 8.2%. Q2: kept at one slot every third cycle.
✓
The guide does NOT interleave — decided, and coded. Only surfing rotates across genres; the guide keeps its genre blocks in all three arms, so a deliberate browse stays scannable and the filter chips behave exactly as they do today. The two orders therefore disagree about what “next” means — accepted knowingly. Mechanically the guide renders from its own list, snapshotted after the within-genre sort and before the interleave, holding the same channel objects so tapping a row still tunes the right channel.
Section 5

Arm RL — how the lock works

The prior behind this arm is the strongest number in the whole design: of users with two sessions, 44.9% returned to the genre they last held, against a 20.8% chance baseline from genre tune-share. Genre affinity is real at 2.2× chance.

  1. Two triggers, whichever fires first — a single tune held ≥ 60s, or ≥ 90s cumulative dwell in one genre across the session.
  2. Checked on a 5-second ticker, not only at the flick. This matters: the list ahead has to already be re-sorted before the user presses down, otherwise the lock lands one flick late and the arm measures nothing.
  3. Re-sort only the slots ahead of the user. The locked genre's remaining channels move to the front of the tail; the rotation resumes behind them. Nothing at or behind the current position ever moves. This is the one bug that would invalidate the arm — the playing channel is an array index, so mutating behind it would silently retarget what is on screen.
  4. Persist and honour. The genre is written to localStorage; a returning user gets that genre first. They still boot to their last-watched channel, not the genre hero — a deliberate deviation from spec_6, see Q4.
  5. One re-lock per session, maximum. If a different genre later crosses the cumulative bar, the lock moves once and then stops. Thrash guard.
!
Declared confound. RL is cross-genre discovery plus personalised persistence. If it wins we will not know which half did it. That is accepted knowingly — it is the behaviour the product would actually ship — and a win pre-commits us to a decomposition follow-up (RL vs RL-without-persistence), not to a claim about mechanism.

Section 7

What happens if the code sits on GitHub un-deployed

The client is static and goes live on git pull; the server only changes after reload-wiom-tv. In July that gap silently destroyed four days of attribution. It cannot happen again, and this has been verified against the real old server.

  • The client refuses to enrol anyone until /api/order returns algo:'v3'. Until then: nobody enrolled, nothing stamped, no interleave.
  • The News landing still works — heroByGenre has shipped since fse_v2 — so an un-deployed merge degrades to exactly Arm RC, which is the incumbent experience.
  • Failure mode is clean exclusion, never corrupted attribution, and it self-heals the moment the server reloads.
  • Verified by running the new client against origin/main's actual old server: 5 checks, all green, inside a 61/61 E2E suite.

Decisions

All ten settled. Nine went with the recommendation; Q6 did not — the guide keeps its genre blocks. Everything here is applied in code on exp/fse-v3-cross-genre.

01Do we ratify promoting News on a secondary?Highest stakes
fse_v2's pre-registered primary returned nothing; News won on watch minutes. Shipping it is a judgement call we have effectively already made — the code hardcodes News. The question is whether it goes on the record as a deliberate deviation, or whether we would rather re-open it. If we ratify, fse_v3's rule 3 ("the incumbent wins all ambiguity") needs a sentence explaining why the same standard was not applied to fse_v2.
Decided Ratify, in writing, in the fse_v2 readout. The effect survived every robustness check and the cost of being wrong is low (News vs Entertainment as a landing). But write down that it was a secondary — otherwise the next person reads fse_v3's rule 3 and concludes we do not follow our own protocol.
02What weight does the Other bucket get?Low stakes
The spec's stated reason was wrong (they are 52 channels, not "1–4 each"), but the conclusion still holds on the numbers. And the choice barely matters where anyone looks:
WeightFirst Other slotOther channels in top 50

One slot in the top 50 separates the live options, and three-quarters of first sessions never pass slot 5.
Decided Keep 1-in-3 and stop analysing it. It is server-owned (ORDER_V3 on /api/order), so it can be retuned on a reload without a client deploy.
03Arm RL's memory dies when localStorage is cleared. Accept?Low stakes
It genuinely cannot survive a clear — there is no server-side identity to hang it on, so the lock is device-scoped and under-counts. The fix is not to make it survive; it is to stop guessing how much is lost.
Decided — already coded Accept device-scoped, but measure the loss. Every returning session now emits a session_order beacon carrying {lock, honoured}. A user who locked and returned with storage intact reports honoured:1; one whose storage cleared reports lock:null. The gap between genre_lock fires and honoured returns is the under-count — observed, not estimated.
04On return, should a locked user boot to the genre hero or their last-watched channel?Medium stakes
spec_6 says the hero. The code currently does last-watched, and I want that deviation to be a decision rather than a default. Last-watched is already inside the locked genre — that is what they locked on — and is a strictly better guess than the genre's population hero. Overriding it adds a third component to an arm that is already compound, and regresses behaviour for exactly the users the arm exists to help.
Decided Keep last-watched. One constant flips it (RL_RETURN_HERO) if you disagree.
05Three-quarters of the cohort never flick far enough to see the treatment. Do we proceed anyway?Highest stakes
This is the risk the spec does not name. spec_6 worries that same-genre hopping might be preference rather than adjacency; the sharper worry is exposure: 38% of first sessions are one tune long. For them RI and RC are byte-identical experiences, and they sit in the denominator of the primary, diluting any true effect toward zero.

Three ways out: (a) run it as specified and accept the dilution — the effect we are powered for (+27%-sized) was measured on the same diluted population, so it is not fatal; (b) pre-register a secondary analysis on the ≥ 2-tune subgroup, knowing it is post-randomisation conditioning and therefore not causal; (c) pair fse_v3 with something that increases flicking at all — which would confound it.
Decided (a) plus (b), stated in advance. Primary stays all-enrolled and intention-to-treat. Add the ≥ 2-tune subgroup as a pre-registered descriptive cut, explicitly labelled non-causal — so we can tell "no effect" apart from "no exposure" without pretending the subgroup is a clean comparison.
06Is a mixed-genre default guide acceptable UX?Medium stakes
Under RI the guide's default scroll would have stopped being genre blocks and become a rotating mix. Every row keeps its genre label and the filter chips still narrow to one genre, so nothing would have been lost — but "scroll to the News block" becomes "tap News". For a user who came for one genre that is a tax; for a user who does not know what is on offer it is the point of the arm.
Decided — against the recommendation Interleave the surf order only; the guide keeps genre blocks. A deliberate browse stays scannable by genre in every arm. The trade accepted: surf order and guide now disagree about what "next" means, and the treatment is confined to passive flicking rather than both surfaces. Coded — and it cost the E2E suite its observable, since the guide DOM was how the tests read the list under test; there is now a localhost-only __surfOrder seam behind the same guard as ?_arm.
07Does Devotional keep its slot in the cycle?Low stakes
Devotional is 41 channels at a 3.6% hold-30 — the weakest of the five — yet round-robin gives it slot 5 of every cycle, which means it appears in the first five slots every user sees. The health gate limits the damage (only verified channels can lead it), but it is the most expensive slot in the design going to the weakest genre.
Decided Keep it for the day-5 look, then decide with data. If the day-5 read shows Devotional slots bleeding, drop it into Other and record it as a protocol deviation. Removing it pre-emptively would bias the rotation toward what we already believe.
08Three arms for 28 days, or two arms for 21?Medium stakes
At ~250 new uids/day, three arms is ~83/arm/day and a 28-day clock. Dropping RL and running RC vs RI at α = 0.05 reaches the same MDE in 21 days — a week faster, and the result would be causally cleaner because RI is a single-component treatment.

Against that: RL carries the only mechanistic prior in the design (44.9% return affinity at 2.2× chance) and is the only arm aimed at the returner gap, which News did not close.
Decided Keep three. The week is worth the arm that targets retention. But this is a genuine trade and worth your call rather than mine — if the next thing in the queue is time-critical, two arms is the defensible choice.

Decided No re-probe — the 24 July run stands. 21 channels stripped in this PR. Every one of the 21 FAILED the probe (404 manifest, not-HLS, no segments, dead segments) and is independently flagged unhealthy by the event data, so this is not a ranking judgement — they cannot play at all.
