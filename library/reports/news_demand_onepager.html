Validated · 27 Jul 2026 Wiom TV · Product proposal

News is not a spike. It is the demand engine.

Two national news events — the 20 July Delhi march and the 25 July resignation — produced the two heaviest watch days in wiom.tv's history. Stripping whales, the lift holds. It is carried by depth, not new installs, and it shows up independently on the Android app. We should stop treating news as one of nine genres and build the News Desk.

The natural experiment

We did not run this test — the news cycle ran it for us. The CJP protest arc gave us two hard-dated demand shocks fourteen days into a stable data era, with no product change in between.

13 Jul

Baseline era begins

PR#8 ships: 5 dead channels removed, gesture-unmute, fse_v2 landing arms go live. Everything after this date is one clean, comparable era — the pre and post windows below sit entirely inside it.

20 Jul

Delhi march · police action

CJP marches on the Education Ministry; 150 protesters injured. Watch time jumps to 26.6 h — the heaviest day in five weeks. News alone takes 45.1% of the day.

News 12.0 h · +212% vs prior 7d
21–24 Jul

The lift does not decay

Marches are suspended, talks with government begin. Watch time stays elevated every single day — 25.7, 19.1, 18.9, 29.4 h. This is the part that matters: a one-day spike would have fallen back. It did not.

25 Jul

Education Minister resigns

48.8 h watched — the single biggest day on record, 3.1× the prior week's average. News is 52.7% of it, concentrated in the 10:00, 15:00 and 23:00 bulletin hours.

News 25.7 h · 6.7× the pre-window daily average

That combination is the whole argument. Reach was flat, so this was not acquisition. The lift survives whale-stripping, so it was not one heavy household. What grew was depth per existing user — and the depth is news-shaped: News took 73% of every incremental hour, and its share of all watch time went from a quarter to nearly a half.

Two independent products, different codebases, different measurement stacks, same week, same shape: whale-independent depth growth, carried by news. On Android the effect is even starker — news absorbed essentially all of the incremental hours, and the app was already 87% news before the shock. Its peak landed on 20–21 Jul rather than 25 Jul, consistent with a TV-in-the-living-room audience following the march coverage rather than the announcement.

The old Android app cannot answer this — and that is a finding, not a gap

The v11/v12 app is retired. It served 2 devices in all of July against 410 on v13, having migrated its base by January 2026. There is no population left on it to test, so every Android number above is the new app. Anyone asking "does this replicate on the old app?" should be told it has no users, not that the test was inconclusive.

Does news retain, or just spike?

Depth only matters if it comes back. Users whose first day includes 2+ minutes of news return at D1 17.5% and D7 27.5% (n = 229) against 12.4% and 24.0% for users who watched 2+ minutes of anything else (n = 840). But that comparison flatters news, because news viewers also watch far longer on day one. Controlling for first-day depth:

Read this honestly. Below five minutes, news is worse than the rest of the catalogue — a user who samples news and bounces is a user we lost. Above five minutes it is consistently 1.6–1.9× better, and the advantage widens with depth. The cell sizes are small (19–65), so this is directional, not conclusive. The design implication is sharp: the prize is not getting people to news, it is getting them past the first five minutes of it.

We are already half-testing this

fse_v2 has been running since 13 Jul with four landing arms, one of which — LN — lands new users directly on the best-performing news channel. It is the closest thing we have to the proposed product, and it is quietly the best arm:

Do not call this a win yet

LN beats control on every metric, but none of it is significant. The survival gap is +3.0pp with a 95% CI of −1.2 to +7.3pp (p = 0.16); the minimum detectable effect at n ≈ 850/arm is 6.0pp. To resolve a real 3pp effect we need roughly 3,370 users per arm — about 40 more days at the current ~61 new users/arm/day. The mechanism is confirmed (LN users watch 1.99 news minutes vs control's 0.61 — the landing works); the payoff is not. *LU's minutes figure is total watch, not news.

What to build

Three pieces, ordered by evidence strength. The first is nearly free and already de-risked; the third is the actual bet.

1 · Keep LN running to significance, and pre-register the call

This week · ~0 eng

The cheapest decision available: do nothing to the code, commit now to a fixed read-out date at n ≈ 3,370/arm (≈ 5 Sep) with 60-second survival as the single primary metric. If we keep peeking we will call a 3pp effect on noise, exactly as we did with fse_v1's arm B.

2 · A News rail that survives the first five minutes

2–3 weeks

The retention table says users who bounce off news inside five minutes are lost, and those who stay are our best cohort. So the surface is not a genre grid — it is a persistent rail of the 4–5 news channels that actually hold attention (Aaj Tak, ABP, NDTV India, India TV, Republic Bharat carried 70% of post-window news hours), with instant switching and no re-buffer penalty. Resume-on-last-news-channel for returning users.

3 · Breaking-news activation

4–6 weeks · the bet

The 20 and 25 July peaks were demand we captured passively. A monitored trigger — a headline band on the player plus a push to installed users when a major story breaks — converts a news cycle we do not control into a return visit we do. This is the only proposal here that grows reach rather than depth, and it is the one that would have made 25 July a 3× day instead of a 3.1× day on a flat user base.

How we will know we were right — and how we would know we were wrong

  • Primary: 60-second first-session survival, LN vs LE, at n = 3,370/arm. Ship if the CI excludes zero.
  • Secondary: user-days ≥ 15 min per active user — the metric that actually moved (1.9×) and the one the rail is designed to move.
  • Guardrail: D1 return for the 2–5 minute news cohort. Today it is 9.2%, below the catalogue. If the rail does not lift this, we have built a better funnel into our worst-retaining experience.
  • Falsifier: if watch hours revert to the 15.7 h/day baseline by mid-August with news share back near 25%, this was a news cycle, not a product truth — and build 3 should not be funded.

The honest caveat

Everything on this page except the fse_v2 table is observational. A national news event moved behaviour; we did not. The correct inference is that latent news demand on this base is far larger than its 25% baseline share suggested — not that a news feature causes a 73% watch-time lift. Build 1 is what converts this from an observation into a claim we can defend.
