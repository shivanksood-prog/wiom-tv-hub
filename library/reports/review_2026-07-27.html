Watch, activation, the July 25 record day & the channels that carry engagement

How every number here is built. Watch = tune-dwell only (sum of tune.duration_sec), capped at 4h per tune to kill left-open-tab outliers (36 such tunes trimmed, −194h); the buggy playback_stall millisecond field is never counted. Unit of analysis is the user (uid), never the session. Days are IST (UTC + 5:30). Only the uid era (from 23 Jun) is used. Genre = the channel's own channel_cat (a real field — no bucketing needed). Activation = a user watching ≥60s total in a day across all channels, over all users who opened the app that day. The current window is a rolling 7 days (Jul 21–27 IST), compared against the prior 7 (Jul 14–20) — the Wed→Tue week is mid-flight, so rolling is the only honest "latest". Meta & YouTube figures in §4 are external pulls, unchanged (Meta Jun 1–Jul 15) — not re-queried today. Era notes: dwell was fixed 27 Jun; a fresh experiment (fse_v2) + 5 dead-channel removals landed 13 Jul — so pre-13-Jul error/unmute rates aren't comparable, but watch-hours are.
01

How the numbers are

The last 7 days are the biggest week on record — 174 hours, up 41% on the prior 7. The driver is not more users (1,779 vs 1,834 — slightly fewer) but far deeper watching by returning users, who now supply 54% of all watch (up from 37% the prior 7 days and 45% in Jul 15–21). The engagement gate is unchanged: only about 1 in 4 users who open the app watch 60 seconds.

What is actually happening: the base has started to compound. Same traffic, far more watch — because returning users now activate at ~35% (vs ~27% a fortnight ago) and stay much longer. News is unambiguously the engine (45% of all watch), and the News landing arm is compounding fastest. Two caveats worth holding: the week contains one extreme day (Jul 25, see §2) and the wall is unchanged — three of four users who land never reach a minute. The first 60 seconds is still the whole game.
How the live dashboard's number differs. The wiom.tv/dashboard tile "Engaged · watched ≥60s" comes from /api/stats in server.js — rendered as funnel.engaged ÷ funnel.opens — and it is not defined the same way as the ~27% activation above. Both count distinct users (uid) and both read the clean tune.duration_sec field (so neither carries the playback_stall ms bug), but the bar and the window differ:
Live dashboard · "Engaged ≥60s"
  • ≥60s in a single tune — any one channel-dwell crosses 60s
  • Window: rolling 24h (UTC), no day boundary
  • Denominator: users who opened (session_start / app_open)
  • No 4h/tune cap
This review · "Activation"
  • ≥60s total across all channels that day
  • Window: IST calendar day
  • Denominator: everyone with any event that day
  • 4h/tune cap applied
Net: the dashboard is stricter on concentration (needs one continuous 60-second sitting) but looser on window & denominator. A user watching 30s each on two channels is activated here but not "engaged" there; a rolling-24h read can also straddle two IST days. So don't quote the live dashboard's % as the same figure — they will differ.
02

What happened on July 25

July 25 is the biggest watch day wiom.tv has ever recorded — 48.7 hours, more than double the ~21h surrounding baseline, from no extra users at all (313 watchers, an ordinary day). It is a news day again, but a different shape from Jul 20: depth, not breadth.

Read this one carefully. Jul 25 is real watch, but it is concentrated, not broad: user count was flat, and five users produced half the day's 48.7 hours (ten produced 74%). Four separate tunes ran long enough to hit the 4h cap — on Aaj Tak HD, ABP News, 9XM and Sanskar TV. Compare Jul 20, where the same news pattern showed up as +27 users on NDTV 24×7, +25 on NDTV Rajasthan, +30 on DD News — genuine breadth. Jul 20 was "many people watched the news"; Jul 25 was "a few people left the news on for hours." Both are real engagement and neither is instrumentation (no deploy landed on either day), but only the Jul 20 shape scales. Treat the 174h week as a true record with a fat tail: normalise Jul 25 to an ordinary day and the week is still ~146h, well clear of the prior 7 days’ 123.8h — the repeat-user trend in §1 does not depend on this one day.

Most-watched channels of the last 7 IST days (Jul 21–27). The LANDING badge marks a default boot / launch-surface channel. One channel has broken away: Aaj Tak HD now carries 29.5h — 17% of all watch on the platform, and it is the only channel where reach and stick both grew.

How to read the buckets. A user is counted once per channel, placed by their total watch time on that channel across the week: >1 min = watched more than 60s (this equals the two depth tiers combined) · 1–5 min = 60s–5min · >5 min = deep watch. "Users (reach)" is everyone who tuned in at all, even for a second — the gap between reach and >1 min is the sub-60s bounce. Δ shows change in >1-min users vs the prior 7 days (Jul 14–20).

Benchmarking — how good are these numbers?

Set the app's depth against two outside yardsticks: the attention we buy on Meta, and the attention Wiom earns organically on YouTube. The app still clears a much higher bar than any ad, and the install gap has widened: installed users now reach 60s on 76% of return days versus 22% for the captive-portal crowd — a 3.4× spread.

Read as directional, not like-for-like. The three denominators differ: Meta = share of paid impressions · YouTube = share of self-selected organic views (a warm, already-engaged base — different from paid) · App = share of user-days reaching 60s. These aren't identical bases, so treat them as benchmarks for "how good is our depth," not a common scoreboard. Because Meta ad videos are sub-60s, ad completion is the fairest Meta analog to our 60-second bar. YouTube figures exclude the 63M-view "Ek kahani" viral outlier.
