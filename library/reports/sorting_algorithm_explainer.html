Two questions, two signals: how channels get ordered

The old algorithm asked one question — “who sticks around?” — and used the answer for everything. But a guide and a landing ask different things, and health was never asked about at all. Every number on this page is live production data from the last 14 days.

What the old version got wrong

Three failures, each observed in production — not hypothetical.

1 · It never looked at whether a channel actually plays. The code assumed errors would “bake in” via short dwells. They didn’t. Shemaroo Josh held rank 1 in Entertainment with 10.7 errors per 100 tunes and a 51% start rate; Hi Dost! sat at rank 3 while half its viewers bailed inside 5 seconds. Appeal outran brokenness.
2 · A coin-flip could outrank a fact. Raw stick-rate treated 7 engaged out of 39 (The Movie Club, 17.9%) as better than 51 out of 347 (Bhojpuri Cinema, 14.7%). This is why the #1 channel churned daily.
3 · One score did two jobs. The same number picked the guide order and the boot channel — but “of those who chose this, who stuck?” is a different question from “of those dropped here cold, who stuck?” They diverge sharply (see below), so one of the two jobs was always being done with the wrong number.

The pipeline

Runs over a rolling 14-day window, cached for 5 minutes, recomputed live — so the order tracks current reality and a recovered channel re-enters on its own.

1

Build the catalog

Channel → genre is derived from event history. Five channels found dead in production (zero successful starts on real traffic) are dropped outright: Travelxp 4K, B4U Movies, B4U Kadak, 9X Jalwa, and a duplicate B4U Music.

→ byGenre{}
2

Judge health — three-valued, and condemnation requires proof

Per channel, over the same window: start-coverage (playback_start / tunes) and error rate (playback_error / tunes). A channel is only condemned when even the charitable reading of its data is bad.

→ BROKEN / UNKNOWN / HEALTHY
3

Score for the guide — stick-rate on chosen tunes

engaged_users / tuners (engaged = watched > 30s), ranked by its Wilson 95% lower bound. Boot tunes are excluded — a boot is not a choice. This is the pollution shield: whatever we land people on can no longer drag its own guide rank around.

→ basePop[ch]
4

Score for the landing — first-impression hold

A user’s first-ever tune to a channel: did it hold ≥30s? Every channel has a sample (it equals its distinct tuner count). The hero of each genre is the best cold performer among healthy channels, needing ≥30 first impressions to qualify. Every landing we serve feeds back into this — so a bad landing demotes itself.

→ heroByGenre[genre]
5

Assemble the order

Within each genre: score = basePop + 2 × (personal signal), then BROKEN channels are pinned to the bottom — no amount of personal affinity can promote a channel with proven bad health into a visible slot. Genres keep their existing slots in the flat list; only the channels inside them are resequenced.

→ { order[], heroByGenre{}, algo:'v2' }

Stage 2 in detail — you need evidence to condemn, not just to promote

The small-sample trap cuts both ways. If a 4-tune channel with 1 error were judged on its raw 25% error rate, we’d execute it on a single unlucky event. So the bar for condemning is the same as the bar for promoting: statistical confidence.

Broken

Pinned to the bottom of its genre. Enough evidence (≥20 tunes) and the charitable reading is still bad: the lower bound of its error rate exceeds 5%, or the upper bound of its coverage falls below 50%.

errLB > 0.05 || covUB < 0.50
Unknown

Explored mid-pack. Thin data — the 4-tunes-1-error case. Can’t be condemned, can’t be a hero. Sits at its genre’s median score, keeps accruing tunes, and graduates automatically as evidence arrives.

tunes < 20, or bounds inconclusive
Healthy

Eligible to rank and to be a hero. Enough evidence and it passes both thresholds outright. Only these channels can hold a top slot or greet a brand-new user.

cov ≥ 0.50 && err ≤ 0.05 && n ≥ 20

In production this pins 20 of 35 Entertainment channels, 22 of 76 News, 13 of 30 Music, and 4 of 12 Movies to the bottom of their genre. That sounds brutal, but it only reorders the tail — and it matches reality: platform-wide start-coverage is about 51%.

Stage 3 in detail — why we rank by the lower bound

A channel with 7 hits out of 39 might be brilliant, or it might be lucky. The Wilson lower bound asks: “what’s the worst this could plausibly be?” — so a channel has to earn a top slot with volume, not luck. Small samples get pulled toward zero; big samples barely move.

Observed rate (what it looks like) Wilson 95% lower bound (what we rank by)

Read the gap as the “luck penalty.” The Movie Club looks like a 14.6% channel on 41 viewers but could plausibly be a 6.9% one — so it ranks as 6.9%. Bhojpuri Cinema claims 15.8% on 355 viewers and keeps 12.4% of it. The apparent winner loses to the proven one, which is exactly the fix for the daily rank-1 churn.

Stage 4 in detail — the two questions really do have different answers

For every channel: how it performs for people who chose it, versus for people meeting it for the first time. If these were the same, one signal would do. They are not.

Chosen — of people who picked it, who stuck Cold — of people meeting it first time, who stuck

Cold is always the harder test (a first impression is harder to win than a deliberate choice), so the levels drop across the board — that’s expected and doesn’t matter. What matters is that the ranking changes. Aaj Tak HD loses 9.4pp when forced on a stranger; Bhojpuri Cinema loses 6.4pp but still holds 9.4% of cold viewers — the best of any healthy Movies channel, which is exactly why it wins that landing slot. Ranking heroes by the purple bar would have optimised the wrong thing.

The cold-start problem — and the rich-get-richer trap

The catalog is built from event history. So a channel we just added has zero events and is invisible to the server.

The trap: if unseen channels were sorted to the bottom (the obvious implementation), they would never be tuned, would never earn events, and would stay buried forever. New content could never break in.

The fix: a channel the server has never seen is placed at its genre’s median slot, not last — mid-pack, where it accrues the data that lets the algorithm judge it. This mirrors the Unknown exploration tier on the server side. Ignorance is treated as ignorance, never as guilt.

Why a bad landing fixes itself

The landing is not a one-way bet — it is a feedback loop, and this is the cushion that makes it safe to try.

Every user we land on a channel becomes one of its first impressions. If they bail before 30 seconds, that is a miss in the denominator and nothing in the numerator — the channel’s cold score falls, its Wilson lower bound falls, and it demotes itself out of the hero slot for the next user.

At the volume a landing arm generates against a channel’s existing sample, a genuinely wrong pick corrects itself in days, not at the end of the experiment. The Wilson lower bound is what stops it whipsawing the other way on one unlucky afternoon.

What does not self-correct is the person who bounced. The population learns; that user is gone. That is the real cost of a bad landing, and the reason the experiment still has an early look and a hard control arm.

Thresholds are judgment calls, not fitted constants. Deliberately strict-first: loosening later quietly promotes channels (harmless), while tightening later demotes them (visible churn for users). At ERR_MAX 0.08, Goldmines and Zee Horror Nights would pass; at START_COV_MIN 0.40, 9XM would. Those are one-line changes when the streams get fixed.

What it deliberately does not do

Known limitations — the honest list.
  • Start-coverage has a zap bias. A user who flips away before the first frame looks the same as a failed start. Mitigated by keeping the floor low (0.5, well under the 0.6–0.9 of healthy channels) and by the error rate, which has no such bias — but it is not a clean measurement.
  • Time-to-first-frame is not used. We log it, but it only exists for tunes that succeeded (~51%) — a survivor-biased number would punish the wrong channels. It needs a playback_giveup event before it can be trusted.
  • No personalisation of the landing. The personal signal needs history, and a brand-new user has none. The landing is deliberately a population-level bet.
  • Thin heroes are possible. DD National HD currently wins Entertainment’s cold slot on just 54 first impressions (13.0%, lower bound 6.4% — a hair above Dangal TV’s 5.7%). Defensible, but thin. This is precisely why the experiment’s control arm is pinned to today’s boot channel rather than to a floating hero.
  • It is not “bug-free.” It is health-aware, small-sample-safe, self-correcting, and covered by 38 automated checks. That is a different and more honest claim.
